# AI Governance Atlas
### From AI Principles to Regulatory Practice

**AI Governance Atlas** is a computational legal research project examining how artificial intelligence governance principles translate into regulatory obligations, institutional accountability and enforcement mechanisms.

The project undertakes a comparative analysis of AI governance frameworks across the **European Union, India and the United Kingdom**, using OECD AI governance principles as an international benchmark.

## Research Objectives

- Examine how different jurisdictions regulate artificial intelligence.
- Identify and compare key AI governance obligations.
- Analyse regulatory approaches to safety, transparency, accountability and human oversight.
- Evaluate institutional responsibilities and enforcement mechanisms.
- Identify gaps between AI governance principles and their practical regulatory implementation.
- Develop a reproducible computational framework for comparative AI regulatory research.

## Jurisdictions and Frameworks

| Framework | Research focus |
|---|---|
| European Union | EU AI Act and risk-based regulation |
| India | National AI governance policies and regulatory instruments |
| United Kingdom | Principles-based AI regulatory framework |
| OECD | International AI governance principles and benchmark |

## Methodology

The project combines computational text analysis with manual legal classification and comparative regulatory research.

The research pipeline includes:

1. **Document Collection:** Gathering regulatory and policy documents from official sources.
2. **PDF Extraction:** Extracting text using PyMuPDF.
3. **Text Preprocessing:** Cleaning extracted text and removing stopwords.
4. **Keyword Analysis:** Examining governance-related terminology and thematic frequencies.
5. **Normalisation:** Calculating keyword frequencies per 1,000 words.
6. **Visualisation:** Generating comparative charts and heatmaps.
7. **Legal Evidence Coding:** Manually classifying selected regulatory provisions.
8. **Legal Verification:** Validating evidence against primary legal sources.
9. **Comparative Analysis:** Examining obligations, accountability, enforcement and implementation.
10. **Research Reporting:** Producing evidence-based findings and academic documentation.

## Research Contribution

AI Governance Atlas aims to move beyond identifying similarities in regulatory language.

Its central research interest is understanding whether and how AI governance principles become concrete, enforceable and institutionally accountable regulatory obligations.

By combining computational analysis with legal interpretation, the project seeks to develop a transparent and reproducible approach to comparative AI governance research.
