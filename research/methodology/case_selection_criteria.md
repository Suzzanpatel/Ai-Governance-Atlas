# AI Governance Atlas

## Case Selection Criteria

### 1. Purpose

This document explains the selection of the three jurisdictional
frameworks examined in AI Governance Atlas---India, the European Union
(EU) and the United Kingdom (UK)---and the use of the OECD AI Principles
as an international benchmark.

The purpose of case selection is to establish a transparent basis for
comparing selected AI governance provisions. The selection is designed
for a focused, document-based comparative study rather than an
exhaustive survey of all AI regulation.

### 2. Selected Frameworks

#### 2.1 India

India is included as a national policy and governance case. Its
inclusion allows the project to examine the form and content of India's
selected AI governance guidance and policy materials, including how
recommendations, institutional responsibilities and governance
expectations are expressed.

The analysis must distinguish policy guidance from binding statutory
obligations. The classification of any individual provision should be
based on its source text and legal context.

#### 2.2 European Union

The EU is included as a regional regulatory framework with a dedicated,
binding AI legislative instrument: the EU Artificial Intelligence Act.

Its inclusion enables examination of a legislative, risk-based approach
and of provisions that specify duties, responsible actors and regulatory
mechanisms. The project analyses only the selected provisions in its
evidence dataset and does not assume that those provisions represent the
entire Act.

#### 2.3 United Kingdom

The UK is included as a national AI governance policy case. Its selected
policy materials allow comparison with the EU's legislative approach and
India's selected guidance.

The analysis distinguishes government policy and regulator-oriented
principles from statutory duties. Any claim about enforceability must be
supported by the specific source and relevant legal context.

#### 2.4 OECD AI Principles --- International Benchmark

The OECD AI Principles are included as an international benchmark, not
as a fourth jurisdiction equivalent to India, the EU or the UK.

They provide an international reference point for considering common AI
governance principles and recommendations. Their non-binding
international character must remain distinct from domestic legislation
and government policy when interpreting comparative results.

### 3. Selection Logic

The four frameworks were selected to enable comparison across different
institutional and legal forms of AI governance:

-   A regional legislative framework (EU).
-   National policy and guidance approaches (India and UK).
-   An international principles-based benchmark (OECD).

This design supports analysis of how governance principles are expressed
in instruments with different legal status and institutional settings.
It also makes it possible to distinguish the existence of a principle
from the creation of a specific obligation, the allocation of
responsibility and the availability of enforcement mechanisms.

These are analytical reasons for the selected comparison. They should
not be read as claims that the four frameworks are equivalent or
directly interchangeable.

### 4. Provision Selection

The current evidence dataset contains 20 selected provisions, with five
records for each framework:

-   India: 5 provisions.
-   European Union: 5 provisions.
-   United Kingdom: 5 provisions.
-   OECD AI Principles: 5 provisions.

The provisions are selected purposively for their relevance to the
project's five governance dimensions:

1.  Transparency
2.  Accountability
3.  Risk Assessment
4.  Safety and Testing
5.  Enforcement

The sample is intended to provide a manageable and structured basis for
comparative coding. It is not a random sample and is not necessarily
representative of the full regulatory or policy framework of any
selected case.

### 5. Inclusion Criteria

A provision may be included where it:

1.  Comes from an official primary legal, regulatory, government-policy
    or international source within one of the selected frameworks.
2.  Has a traceable source reference and identifiable text, article,
    section or principle.
3.  Is relevant to at least one of the five governance dimensions.
4.  Contains a governance principle, requirement, recommendation,
    institutional responsibility or enforcement-related mechanism that
    can be analysed.
5.  Can be classified using the project's coding framework, or its
    classification uncertainty can be explicitly recorded.

### 6. Exclusion and Caution Criteria

A document or provision should not be treated as a verified legal
obligation merely because it discusses AI governance.

The following cautions apply:

-   General commentary, media coverage and secondary summaries should
    not replace the primary source when coding a provision.
-   Aspirational statements should not be coded as mandatory obligations
    unless the source and legal context support that interpretation.
-   A policy recommendation should not be described as binding
    legislation.
-   A provision should not be assigned an implementation status without
    documented implementation evidence.
-   Where the source text or legal status is unclear, the record should
    be flagged for further verification rather than classified by
    assumption.

### 7. Comparability

The frameworks differ in legal force, institutional structure, scope and
instrument type. Comparisons should therefore focus on clearly defined
analytical categories, including:

-   Legal force.
-   Obligation force.
-   Obligation nature.
-   Primary and secondary governance dimensions.
-   Responsible actors.
-   Enforcement mechanisms.
-   Implementation evidence and status.

The project should compare like with like where possible, while
explicitly explaining material differences between legislative duties,
government policy and international recommendations.

The OECD benchmark should be analysed as an international reference
instrument, not included in jurisdictional conclusions as though it were
a national legal system.

### 8. Limitations of Case Selection

The selection is purposive and limited to four frameworks and 20
provisions. It does not cover every AI-related law, regulation, policy,
regulator or institutional practice in India, the EU, the UK or the
OECD.

The five provisions selected for each framework do not necessarily have
identical scope, legal effect or institutional function. Equal record
counts create a balanced dataset for the project's descriptive tables,
but do not establish substantive equivalence or statistical
representativeness.

The findings should consequently be framed as conclusions about the
selected documentary sample. Broader claims about the overall
effectiveness of a framework require additional evidence beyond this
sample.

### 9. Review and Documentation

For each selected provision, retain:

-   A unique record identifier.
-   The framework or jurisdiction.
-   The official source title and issuing body.
-   The article, section or principle reference.
-   The relevant text or a clearly identified extract.
-   The reason for inclusion and its governance dimension.
-   The source-verification decision.
-   Any later coding changes and their rationale.

If the evidence pack is expanded or a provision is replaced, update this
document and the dataset so that the selection process remains
transparent and reproducible.
