# AI Governance Atlas

## Research Questions

### 1. Central Research Question

To what extent do India, the European Union and the United Kingdom
translate AI governance principles into specific, measurable and
enforceable regulatory obligations?

The OECD AI Principles are used as an international benchmark to
contextualise the comparison. The OECD is not treated as a fourth
jurisdiction equivalent to India, the EU or the UK.

### 2. Supporting Research Questions

**RQ1 --- Regulatory approaches**

How do India, the European Union and the United Kingdom approach the
regulation and governance of artificial intelligence?

**RQ2 --- Legal obligations**

To what extent do the selected frameworks translate AI governance
principles into specific and legally binding obligations, and how does
this differ across binding legislation, government policy and
non-binding guidance?

**RQ3 --- Governance dimensions**

How do the selected frameworks address the five governance dimensions
examined in this project: transparency, accountability, risk assessment,
safety and testing, and enforcement?

**RQ4 --- Institutional accountability and enforcement**

What responsibilities are assigned to governments, regulatory
authorities, AI developers, providers and deployers, and what
enforcement or oversight mechanisms are identified in the selected
provisions?

**RQ5 --- Computational methods**

How can natural language processing and computational text analysis
support the identification and comparison of AI governance themes in
official documents, and what are the limitations of these methods when
assessing legal meaning and regulatory effectiveness?

### 3. Research Objectives

1.  Examine the AI governance approaches adopted by India, the EU and
    the UK.
2.  Compare their approaches to transparency, accountability, risk
    assessment, safety and testing, and enforcement.
3.  Identify differences between voluntary governance principles and
    legally binding obligations.
4.  Use natural language processing (NLP) and computational text
    analysis to identify patterns in official AI policy and regulatory
    documents.
5.  Evaluate the limitations of using computational methods to assess
    regulatory effectiveness.

### 4. Scope of the Research

**Frameworks examined**

-   India
-   European Union
-   United Kingdom

**International benchmark**

-   OECD AI Principles

**Governance dimensions**

1.  Transparency
2.  Accountability
3.  Risk Assessment
4.  Safety and Testing
5.  Enforcement

**Unit of analysis**

The current evidence dataset contains 20 selected provisions, five from
each of the four frameworks represented in the dataset: India, the EU,
the UK and the OECD benchmark. The sample is purposive and is not
intended to represent every provision in any framework.

### 5. Relationship Between the Questions and the Research Design

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Research question                   Main analytical focus
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  RQ1                                 Comparative description of the
                                      selected governance approaches

  RQ2                                 Legal force and obligation force

  RQ3                                 Primary and secondary governance
                                      dimensions

  RQ4                                 Responsible actors and enforcement
                                      mechanisms

  RQ5                                 Document processing, NLP, keyword
                                      analysis and methodological
                                      limitations
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The questions are addressed through documentary analysis, computational
text analysis and manual legal and policy coding. Computational patterns
are treated as descriptive evidence and are not, by themselves, proof of
legal enforceability or implementation.

### 6. Expected Contribution

The project aims to develop an evidence-based comparison of how selected
AI governance frameworks translate principles into regulatory
obligations and institutional mechanisms. It also demonstrates how
computational text analysis can support, but not replace, qualitative
legal interpretation.

### 7. Research Limitations

-   The evidence pack is a purposive sample of 20 selected provisions
    and is not necessarily representative of each complete framework.
-   The frameworks differ in legal status, institutional structure,
    document type, scope, length and publication date.
-   Keyword and NLP methods may miss legal context, qualifications,
    nuance and differences in terminology.
-   Legal classification requires manual interpretation of the relevant
    primary-source provisions.
-   Implementation conclusions depend on the availability and quality of
    documentary evidence. Lack of located evidence does not necessarily
    establish non-implementation.
-   The selected sample and methods cannot, by themselves, establish the
    real-world effectiveness of an entire AI governance system.
