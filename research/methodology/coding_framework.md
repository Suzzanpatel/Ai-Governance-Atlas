# AI Governance Atlas

## Legal Provision Coding Framework

### 1. Purpose

This framework establishes consistent classification criteria for the 20
legal and policy provisions examined in AI Governance Atlas.

It aims to improve coding transparency, consistency, comparability and
reproducibility.

### 2. Legal Force

Each provision must be classified according to its legal status.

-   **Binding:** Legally enforceable legal obligation.
-   **Non-binding:** Recommendation or guidance without direct binding
    legal force.
-   **Government policy:** Official government policy that does not
    itself create statutory obligations.
-   **International recommendation:** Non-binding international
    instrument.

**Coding rule:** Classify the legal instrument's status separately from
the force of any individual obligation described in it. Do not assume
that every statement in a binding instrument creates an independently
enforceable duty.

### 3. Obligation Force

-   **Mandatory:** Explicit requirement imposed on an actor.
-   **Recommended:** Express recommendation or expectation.
-   **Voluntary:** Optional commitment or undertaking.
-   **Not specified:** Insufficient evidence to classify.

**Coding rule:** Base this classification on the wording and legal
context of the specific provision. Do not infer a mandatory obligation
from policy aspirations or general principles. Record uncertainty where
the text does not clearly establish the obligation's force.

### 4. Obligation Nature

-   **Institutional:** Responsibility assigned to an institution or
    regulatory authority.
-   **Procedural:** Requirement concerning a process.
-   **Substantive:** Requirement concerning conduct, outcomes or
    standards.
-   **Reporting:** Disclosure, notification or reporting duty.
-   **Other:** Obligation outside the above categories.

A provision may have more than one obligation-nature classification.

**Coding rule:** Select all categories directly supported by the
provision. Where multiple categories are selected, explain the textual
basis for each in the coding rationale. Do not use obligation nature as
a substitute for governance dimension or legal force.

### 5. Governance Dimensions

Each provision receives one primary dimension:

-   **Risk Assessment**
-   **Transparency**
-   **Accountability**
-   **Safety and Testing**
-   **Enforcement**

Secondary dimensions may be assigned where supported by the provision
text.

**Primary-dimension rule:** The primary governance dimension must
reflect the provision's principal regulatory purpose, rather than every
possible consequence of the provision. Secondary dimensions may capture
additional functions where supported by the text.

**Consistency rule:** Apply the same interpretation of each dimension
across all four frameworks. If a provision could reasonably fit more
than one dimension, select the dimension most directly reflected in its
operative requirement and document the reason in the coding rationale.
Do not change a classification solely to make jurisdictional counts look
balanced.

### 6. Implementation Status

-   **Implemented**
-   **Partially implemented**
-   **Not yet implemented**
-   **No evidence of implementation found**
-   **Not assessed**

Implementation classifications must be supported by documented evidence.

**Evidence requirements:** For every assessed implementation status,
record:

1.  The evidence source and issuing organisation.
2.  The publication or access date.
3.  The relevant page, paragraph, section or URL.
4.  The specific evidence supporting the status.
5.  A short explanation connecting the evidence to the classification.

**Interpretation rules:**

-   **Implemented:** Evidence demonstrates that the relevant measure has
    been put into operation.
-   **Partially implemented:** Evidence demonstrates some
    implementation, but also identifies incomplete coverage, outstanding
    steps or material limitations.
-   **Not yet implemented:** Evidence establishes that implementation
    has not started or that the relevant measure remains pending.
-   **No evidence of implementation found:** A documented search was
    conducted, but no adequate implementation evidence was located. This
    does not prove that implementation has not occurred.
-   **Not assessed:** Implementation has not been evaluated. It must not
    be interpreted as failure, non-compliance or absence of
    implementation.

Do not infer implementation merely from the adoption or publication of a
law, strategy, recommendation or policy. Where evidence is insufficient,
use **Not assessed** rather than making an unsupported conclusion.

### 7. Responsible Actor

Identify the relevant actor where supported:

-   **Government**
-   **Regulatory authority**
-   **AI developer**
-   **AI deployer**
-   **Provider**
-   **Other specified institution**

More than one actor may be recorded where the provision allocates
distinct responsibilities to different actors.

**Coding rule:** Distinguish the actor on whom the provision places the
legal or policy responsibility from other actors who may support,
supervise or be affected by its implementation. Use the terminology of
the source instrument where possible. Do not treat provider, developer
and deployer as interchangeable unless the source supports that
equivalence.

### 8. Enforcement Mechanism

Identify whether the provision establishes or is connected to:

-   **Regulatory supervision**
-   **Administrative penalties**
-   **Civil or judicial remedies**
-   **Reporting or notification requirements**
-   **Auditing or conformity assessment**
-   **No identified enforcement mechanism**

**Coding rule:** Distinguish a mechanism expressly established by the
provision from a wider enforcement mechanism elsewhere in the same
instrument or legal framework. Where enforcement arises from another
article, identify that article separately in the source reference or
coding rationale. Do not imply that a recommendation has a legally
enforceable penalty where the source does not establish one.

### 9. Coding Principles

1.  Use official primary sources.
2.  Preserve exact legal references.
3.  Do not infer legal obligations from policy aspirations.
4.  Distinguish binding law from non-binding guidance.
5.  Record uncertainty rather than making unsupported classifications.
6.  Preserve original coding decisions and document all subsequent
    changes.
7.  Allow multiple obligation-nature classifications where justified.
8.  Separate legal force, obligation force, obligation nature,
    governance dimension, implementation status, responsible actor and
    enforcement mechanism.
9.  Record a concise rationale for material or potentially contestable
    coding decisions.
10. Use comparable coding criteria across jurisdictions while respecting
    differences in legal systems, institutional structures and
    instrument types.
11. Do not interpret missing evidence as evidence of non-implementation.
12. Retain a traceable source reference for each coded provision and
    each implementation claim.

### 10. Limitations

The evidence pack contains 20 selected provisions, five from each
framework.

The sample is purposive and is not necessarily representative of the
entire regulatory framework of each jurisdiction.

Comparative findings must account for differences in legal force,
institutional structure and implementation evidence.

The selected provisions support a structured comparison of the included
examples; they do not, by themselves, establish that one jurisdiction's
entire AI governance system is more effective than another's.

### 11. Review and Change Log

For each reviewed record, retain:

-   Record ID.
-   Original classification.
-   Revised classification, if any.
-   Reason for the change.
-   Primary source reference.
-   Reviewer decision and review date.

Use **Needs source check** where the primary source or relevant passage
has not yet been verified. Use **Verified** only after the source text
and the coding rationale have been checked. A verified legal
classification does not automatically verify an implementation-status
claim; implementation evidence must be assessed separately.
