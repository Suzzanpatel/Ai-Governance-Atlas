## 4. Comparative interpretation framework

The results should be interpreted through four separate analytical layers:

1. **Formal legal framework:** whether the relevant instrument or provision is binding, and the legal status applicable to the specific duty.
2. **Substantive obligation:** what conduct is required, permitted, restricted, or recommended, and which actor is addressed.
3. **Institutional and enforcement design:** which bodies supervise, administer, investigate, or enforce the relevant requirements, and what remedies or sanctions are available.
4. **Implementation evidence:** whether reliable, dated and attributable sources demonstrate operational steps, institutional activity, or observed implementation.

These layers must not be collapsed into a single measure of regulatory strength or effectiveness.

## 5. Research questions for the final discussion

- Which governance dimensions are represented across all jurisdictions in the selected sample, and which are unevenly represented?
- How do binding obligations differ from voluntary, policy-based, or recommendatory approaches?
- Are duties and supervisory functions clearly allocated among providers, deployers, regulators, and public authorities?
- Do the selected provisions provide identifiable enforcement routes, and are those routes directly linked to the duties analysed?
- What implementation evidence is available, and where does the evidence base remain incomplete?
- What additional provisions or primary sources are required before making broader jurisdiction-level conclusions?

## 6. Limitations and safeguards

- The dataset contains a selected sample of 20 provisions; it is not a comprehensive inventory of the four jurisdictions or benchmark groups.
- Counts and percentages are affected by the selection and coding of provisions.
- A category absent from the sample is not necessarily absent from the jurisdiction's legal or policy framework.
- Recorded enforcement mechanisms do not demonstrate that enforcement has occurred.
- Recorded implementation status and notes are not independent proof of implementation.
- Phase 12 pending corrections have not been automatically applied.
- This script does not conduct source retrieval, legal interpretation, or independent verification.

## 7. What is needed before drawing the final research conclusions

1. Review each jurisdiction's primary legal sources for the provisions that support the comparative claims.
2. Check whether legal status and commencement dates are current.
3. Verify the relationship between substantive duties and enforcement provisions, including cross-references elsewhere in the instrument.
4. Support implementation claims with dated official sources and specific passages.
5. Expand the provision sample where a jurisdiction or governance dimension is underrepresented.
6. Record unresolved or contradictory evidence transparently.

## 8. Conclusion status

This phase produces a consolidated descriptive findings document. It does not automatically generate a definitive conclusion about which jurisdiction has the strongest, weakest, or most effective AI governance system. Such conclusions require a defensible comparison framework, verified primary-source analysis, and evidence of implementation.
