## 4. Comparative interpretation

Use the jurisdiction-level percentage tables to compare the composition of each jurisdiction's selected sample. Use the pairwise cross-tabulations to identify which labels co-occur in the dataset. These are descriptive relationships, not legal conclusions.

A binding instrument may contain provisions with different kinds of duties and different enforcement routes. Conversely, a non-binding instrument may influence governance without creating directly enforceable legal obligations. The dataset's labels must therefore be checked against the relevant source text before making instrument-wide claims.

## 5. Questions for legal interpretation

1. Is the recorded legal status accurate for the specific provision, rather than only for the instrument as a whole?
2. Does the text create a mandatory duty, a conditional duty, a power, a recommendation, or a voluntary commitment?
3. Who is legally bound, and under what conditions does the duty apply?
4. Is the recorded enforcement mechanism directly attached to this provision, located elsewhere in the instrument, or supplied by another legal framework?
5. Are there procedural, jurisdictional, or commencement conditions that affect enforceability?

## 6. Limitations

- The script does not read or interpret primary legal sources.
- The script does not determine whether an instrument is legally binding as a matter of law.
- A recorded enforcement mechanism does not demonstrate actual enforcement.
- Co-occurrence in a dataset does not establish a legal relationship.
- The 20 provisions are a selected sample and do not represent every relevant rule in each jurisdiction.
- Pending Phase 12 corrections have not been applied.
